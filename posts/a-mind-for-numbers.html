Chapter 2 — Easy does it

Posted on 2026-08-19

Hooks of knowledge before the deep reading

The chapter starts by giving us an insight about how to learn: abstracts, images, subtitles and the questions at the end of a chapter help us before we start to read the content deeply, creating hooks of knowledge in the brain and relating the content with images and questions previously seen.

The two modes of thinking

It then presents the two modes of thinking, focused and diffuse, between which we alternate in our daily lives.

Mode When we use it What happens
Focused when we are reasoning something out we use the prefrontal cortex to solve problems directly, like a calculation while we are looking at it
Diffuse when we wake up or take a shower we resolve things and have insights because the brain processes all of this unconsciously

But the diffuse mode only works once we have already been introduced to the material — in other words, the diffuse mode only works after we have first read the problem in the focused mode.

Why the exact sciences feel harder

Mathematics and the sciences tend to be more challenging due to their abstractions; for instance, the "+" symbol does not exist physically — it is merely a convention.

Chapter 3 — Learning is creating

Posted on 2026-08-27

Easy ways to switch between the two modes

There are some easy ways to switch between the focused and the diffuse modes of thinking, such as:

  • taking a bus;
  • taking a shower;
  • going for a walk;
  • taking a nap.

To activate the diffuse mode it is necessary to be focused on something else, like the things mentioned above, or to relax the brain and try to "not think about anything".

Fixing content in your mind

To fix some content in your mind, it is necessary to use the working memory — that is, the short-term memory — to repeat that content over some days or weeks. This is what makes your brain fix the content in your long-term memory.

When you are stuck on a problem

And if you are stuck on some difficult problem, try to focus on another problem. By doing that, your diffuse way of thinking will keep making connections in the background. And in other cases, try to take a nap for 20 minutes — this is the right amount of time to "reboot" your brain.

Chapter 4 — Chunking and avoiding illusions of competence

Posted on 2026-09-01

The reporter who remembered everything

The chapter starts by telling the story of a Soviet reporter who had a super memory about so many events in his life, but there was a problem: his brain was conditioned to keep the smallest details about the things he saw, and he could not create processing chunks — which are basically the puzzle the brain builds about things and their relations.

How to create chunks

# Step What it means
1 Stay in the focused mode simply keep the focused mode on your problem, away from the television, the phone and anything else that distracts you. If you can focus, the brain starts to create the connections with the content that already exists in it, and the learning process begins
2 Understand the fundamentals understand why you are learning that and what it solves. It is necessary to practice it a little to really learn what it does
3 Gain context understand when to use it. In the long term this gives you solid knowledge of maths or anything else, and it helps you improve and know how to solve a problem only because you have a broad context of so many contents

Illusions of competence

This chapter also discusses how it is practically useless to underline and mark the text of a book, unless you emphasize the key points of the text and come back a few times to reread them. That is what makes the content stick in your mind, gives you solid knowledge and helps you find the holes you do not understand yet.

Furthermore, creating chunks of knowledge helps you solve problems in the future that you have never seen before, because your brain has so many connections between chunks that your overall knowledge goes up, like I said above.

It is also recommended to reserve some time during the week for general reading (journals, magazines, etc.) in order to create more chunks of knowledge and to give yourself broader creativity and innovation.

Another thing that helps you fix the content is going back to it a few times — but not only rereading it: practice the activities and the contents, because that is what solidifies them in your brain. A more advanced tip is to draw diagrams about all the content you learn, making physical connections between the pieces.

The interleaving technique

It is not that useful to do only one type of activity about your content for hours, because when you are doing the same kind of calculation over and over you are not learning something, you are only memorizing it. The right approach is to do one kind of problem, then move to another, come back to the first kind, then go for another one, and so it goes.

The key point is that during the test you do not know which approach to use to solve each question, so you have to find the patterns.

Chapter 6 — Zombies everywhere

Posted on 2026-09-19

The four parts of a habit

This chapter talks about that one subject that is procrastination and productivity. It starts with the four parts that build any habit, good or bad.

Part What it is
Cue the trigger, what happens in our day-to-day that makes us repeat a habit, both the good and the bad ones
Routine the action you take right after the cue
Reward what you get out of the routine. In a bad habit it is usually a temporary sensation of pleasure, and that is what makes the habit so hard to let go of
Belief when your mind believes there is no way to let go of that bad habit

The Pomodoro technique

Further on the chapter discusses the Pomodoro method, which consists of a 25 minute timer that gives you 5 minutes of rest afterwards. This is a method I personally do not like, because when you are in those 5 minutes of rest and do something pleasurable, like using the phone, going back to the hard work and keeping the focus on it is very hard.

Focus on the process, not on the product

It also discusses not keeping the focus on the product but on the process, which is what causes the lack of willpower. For example: when you tell your mind that you need to do some homework that is hard, you do not want to do it, because you are anticipating the pain. Instead of that, tell your mind that you need to keep the focus on the homework for 25 minutes, and it will end up that you do the homework at the end.

Have a finish line

Finally, the chapter discusses having a finish line, which gives you constant and visible progress, and that is what helps you to actually do something.

Chapter 7 — Chunking versus choking

Posted on 2026-10-05

The chapter starts by discussing how so many inventions come from an upgrade of an old idea.

How to build strong chunks

Then it explains how to build strong chunks of knowledge, step by step.

Step What to do
1 solve the problem end-to-end, and if you have the solution, don't look at it, do it your own way
2 do another problem, paying attention to the key points
3 take a pause, you can study other aspects of your content
4 sleep, but before you sleep, solve another problem and let your subconscious work on the hard parts that you don't understand
5 after waking up, do another repetition, and you will realize that it becomes a little easier
6 choose a new problem and repeat steps 1 to 5 again
7 do some active repetitions, solving the old problems again

Active review

The chapter discusses again the importance of actively reviewing old problems, which gives you a solid and fixed chunk of knowledge.

Knowledge doesn't grow gradually

It is normal that knowledge doesn't grow in our mind in a gradual way. Some things that made sense before may not make sense anymore later, and vice versa.

Practice by hand for the exam

To remember the content in the exam, it is recommended to do the problems by hand with a pen or pencil. It helps to fix the content and to train the brain for the exam.
