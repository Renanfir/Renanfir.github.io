Chapter 2 — Easy does it

Hooks of knowledge before the deep reading

The chapter starts by giving us an insight about how to learn: abstracts, images, subtitles and the questions at the end of a chapter help us before we start to read the content deeply, creating hooks of knowledge in the brain and relating the content with images and questions previously seen.

The two modes of thinking

It then presents the two modes of thinking, focused and diffuse, between which we alternate in our daily lives.

Mode When we use it What happens
Focused when we are reasoning something out we use the prefrontal cortex to solve problems directly, like a calculation while we are looking at it
Diffuse when we wake up or take a shower we resolve things and have insights because the brain processes all of this unconsciously

But the diffuse mode only works once we have already been introduced to the material — in other words, the diffuse mode only works after we have first read the problem in the focused mode.

Why the exact sciences feel harder

Mathematics and the sciences tend to be more challenging due to their abstractions; for instance, the "+" symbol does not exist physically — it is merely a convention.
